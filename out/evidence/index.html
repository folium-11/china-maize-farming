How I checked my evidence

Besides data sets, I added four main sources after my progress report: the 2025 Heilongjiang bulletin (HPBS 2026), two farmer surveys (Huo et al. 2022, Yu et al. 2025) and a review of black soil governance (Liao et al. 2026). The surveys cover the social side, which was the weak part of my research log.

Comparison table

Table 1 follows the plan in my progress report, with an extra row for who paid.

Along the first row, government sources describe progress, the programme’s scientists describe both damage and success, and independent sources are the most cautious. None shows the black layer as a whole getting thicker.

Concept map

The map showed me that the two most positive papers share authors and funding, and that nearly all my Chinese statistics come from one government system.

Limits of my evidence

  • Language. My parents helped me translate the bulletins and I checked every number against the Chinese, using DeepL for less important pages. I found no first-hand interviews with Heilongjiang corn farmers, so farmers’ views come from two surveys, one in Jilin.
  • Forecasts. The USDA forecasts in my progress report were wrong or have changed. IndexMundi (n.d.) still showed an old 13 million tonne forecast for 2024, so I used USDA’s later 1.8 million. The income figure in Huo et al. (2022) is older and much lower than USDA’s.
  • Phone check. Figure 3 was unreadable on my phone, so I split it into two panels with a narrow version.
