4. What corn means for rural communities

What impacts does corn cultivation cause economically and socially within rural communities at a time when the province’s population is decreasing?

Rural incomes are rising for the people who remain. Rural disposable income rose 5.8 per cent in 2025 to 22,188 yuan per person (HPBS 2026). Farms on the Northeast Plain average 12.8 hectares per person, compared with 2.7 on the North China Plain (Huo et al. 2022), so a profit of about US$436 per hectare adds up.

A shrinking, ageing population

Heilongjiang lost 330,000 people in 2024 (Caixin Global 2025) and 280,000 in 2025, and by my calculation its rural population fell by about 370,000 that year as urbanisation rose from 68.05 to 68.98 per cent (HPBS 2025, 2026). More than half the region’s primary schools have closed in a decade (Caixin Global 2025), giving young families less reason to stay.

On the Northeast Plain, 40.3 per cent of farmers surveyed were over 54 and only 10.6 per cent were under 35 (Huo et al. 2022). In Lishu County in neighbouring Jilin, the average farmer surveyed was 58 (Yu et al. 2025).

Do machines replace people?

My progress report suggested that people leave first and machines follow. This is only half right. The large, gently sloping farms suit machines, but Northeast Plain farmers ranked a lack of machinery training as their biggest barrier, and a machine costs US$1,500 to US$15,000 when cereal farmers earned under US$181.49 per hectare (Huo et al. 2022). Many hire machine services from cooperatives instead.

Only 54 per cent of the Lishu farmers had adopted conservation tillage, and some of the others were ‘informed non-adopters’ who understood it but were not convinced (Yu et al. 2025). I think this ageing, hesitant workforce is the pressure being handled least well.
