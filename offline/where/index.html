1. Where the corn grows and why

Where is corn cultivated in Heilongjiang, and why do the physical conditions of that province cause high corn yields?

China’s corn is concentrated in the north (Figure 1). Heilongjiang grew 15.7 per cent of it in 2025 (HPBS 2026, NBS 2025), and with Jilin, Liaoning and Inner Mongolia it forms a corn belt growing 45.5 per cent (NBS 2026).

Inside Heilongjiang, the Songnen Plain in the south-west is an important grain producing area (Sui and Dai 2025), but without county data my map stops at the provincial scale.

Physical conditions

Black soils have a thick, dark topsoil rich in organic matter (Liao et al. 2026), and the cold climate slows the microbes that break it down (Jiang et al. 2025), so it builds up. The gently rolling Songnen Plain suits large machines, and most rain falls in summer, when corn grows.

However, the output comes more from scale than yield. Heilongjiang’s yield of 7.09 tonnes per hectare was only 6 per cent above the national average, and its share of China’s corn area (14.8 per cent) is close to its share of output (HPBS 2026, NBS 2025).
