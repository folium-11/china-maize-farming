5. Is the management working?

How effective are the current management practices and what improvements are possible?

China’s Black Soil Protection Law came into force on 1 August 2022 (Xinhua 2022), and Liao et al. (2026) call it the world’s first. Xinhua (2022), the state news agency, reported it as a success, quoting one supportive farmer and leaving out costs. A national plan aimed to cover 9.33 million hectares with conservation tillage by 2025 (Xinhua 2022), and the best-known method is the Lishu Model of leaving straw on the surface and planting without ploughing (Jiang et al. 2025).

What is working

Straw covering a third of the surface cuts erosion by about 80 per cent and two thirds cover by about 95 per cent, and the arable layer in monitored areas thickened from 19.8 to 23.3 centimetres between 2014 and 2019 (Sui and Dai 2025). This seemed to contradict page 2, but the arable layer is the part farmers till, so deeper ploughing can thicken it while the black layer keeps thinning.

Claims that need checking

The biggest claims come from the programme’s own scientists, such as over 80 per cent less erosion from Lishu Model 2.0 (Jiang et al. 2025) and over 15 per cent higher maize yields (Liao et al. 2026). These papers share authors and both grant numbers start with XDA28, the same Chinese Academy of Sciences programme, so one does not really confirm the other. The independent study found that no till with full straw cover lowered maize yields in Jilin (Sui and Dai 2025), which may explain the low adoption on page 4.

The plan also covers only about a quarter of the 36 million hectares of cultivated black soil (Jiang et al. 2025), and I found no figures on enforcement.

Possible improvements

  • Farmers teaching farmers, as Yu et al. (2025) recommend. On Heilongjiang’s Sanjiang Plain, farmers adopted a new fertiliser method only after trusted neighbours showed them it was easy (Liao et al. 2026).
  • Machinery training and shared machines through cooperatives, since training was the biggest barrier (Huo et al. 2022).
  • Yearly measurement of the black layer by a group outside the programme.

Conclusion

Overall, corn farming on Heilongjiang’s black soils is not sustainable yet, because output keeps rising while the black layer keeps thinning. Environmental pressures are managed well where the methods are used, but the plan’s reach and the lack of independent checking are weaknesses. Social pressures are handled worst, since fewer and older farmers are expected to adopt methods and machines without training.

Conservation tillage works at the field scale but covers only part of the province, while the national grain target pressures every hectare. As I said in my progress report, sustainability depends on who describes it. The NBS reports growing harvests, while soil scientists describe a resource that is slowly running out.
