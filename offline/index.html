Corn farming on the black soils of Heilongjiang Province, China

Maize, or corn, supplies 42.1 per cent of China’s grain, and Heilongjiang grows more of it than any other province (NBS 2025, 2026). Most of it grows on black soil, one of the world’s most fertile soils, which is slowly being worn away.

Main research question

How sustainable is the cultivation of maize in the black soils of Heilongjiang province, and how well is the mitigation of the social and environmental pressures caused by such cultivation handled?

My answer in short

Not sustainable yet. Output is rising, but the black topsoil underneath is still thinning. Soil protection works where farmers use it, but the farmers being asked to use it are older and fewer every year.
