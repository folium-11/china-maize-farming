Corn farming on the black soils of Heilongjiang Province, China

Maize, or corn, supplies 42.1 percent of China’s grain, and Heilongjiang is consistently leading in harvests and produces more than any other province (NBS 2025, 2026). Most of the corn grown in Heilongjiang is on black soil, a kind of earth that is considered one of the most fertile in the world. However, this black soil is slowly being worn away by intense farming practices, which is what will be investigated.

Main research question

How sustainable is the cultivation of maize in the black soils of Heilongjiang province, and how well is the mitigation of the social and environmental pressures caused by such cultivation handled?

My answer

Considerably unsustainable. The harvest of corn is increasing but the resultant effect is the topsoil underneath thinning. A promising solution is soil protection, however, the current implementation rate is very low and still ever decreasing.
